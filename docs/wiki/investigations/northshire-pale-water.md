# Northshire pale water

Northshire's streams rendered as flat, pale white-blue sheets. The Godot water shader was the Bevy procedural placeholder. It used a generated normal map, constant base and sky colours, a hard-coded light direction and a fixed 0.08–0.85 alpha. It also never received MH2O vertex depths. Water now ports the retail water material from WebWowViewerCpp `liquidWaterMat.slang` (`calcWaterLiquidMat`), with inputs from DB2 and LightData.

## Causes and fixes

1. **Vertex depths were dropped.** Northshire's layers are LiquidType 5 "Slow Water" through LiquidObject 427. Its LiquidMaterial 1 uses LVF 0: all heights first, then all depth bytes (wowdev MH2O; WebWowViewerCpp `LiquidDataGetters.h` `getLiquidDepth`). The parser read only the heights, so every vertex got depth 1.0. `adt_tex_water.rs` now also reads the depths. Tile 32_48 has depths from 0 to 80, and depth 0 is authored on dry cells.
2. **No LiquidType material.** Colours and normals were constants. `scripts/export_db2_csv.py` now exports LiquidType, LiquidObject, LiquidMaterial and LiquidTypeXTexture from local CASC 12.1.0.69933. `game_engine_core::liquid_data::LiquidCatalog` resolves each layer following `LiquidMaterialManager.cpp`: the object gives the type, the type gives the material and LVF, and the texture slots are filled by `FrameCountTexture`. For type 5 that is slot 2 bump `newoceanbump.blp` (463849), slot 3 foam `waterchop7bw.blp` (317230), and River colours (procedural texture type 1). The shader uses Float[0..17], Coefficient[0..3] and the LiquidObject flow values. Slot 0's 30 `lake_a` frames are not read by the modern water shader.
3. **Wrong colour source.** The shader now uses the LightData RiverClose/Far (or OceanClose/Far) colours at the camera, with LightParams Water/Ocean Shallow/Deep alphas blended across the same LightParams weights (`DayNightLightHolder.cpp:928-937`, `MapSceneRenderer.cpp:194-203`). The specular colour is SunColor. Underwater fog comes from the Light's underwater LightParams slot and is inert when that slot has no keyframes (`MapSceneRenderer.cpp:297-315`).
4. **Wrong blend.** The water now reads Godot's depth and screen textures. The scene behind the water is refracted and underwater-fogged by the water's thickness, then mixed with the lit close/far colour using the depth-cubic alpha. Shore alpha and foam fade over the first yards of thickness, and depth-0 vertices are invisible.

## Other liquids

Every LiquidMaterial now has its shader, selected as in `LiquidMaterialManager.cpp` `createLiquidMaterial` (`liquid_data::LiquidShader`): 1/3 and unlisted IDs water, 2/4 magma and slime (`liquid_magma.gdshader`, with the 128x128x16 noise volume `xtextures/fx/noise.blob` 768431), 5 mercury, 10 fog, 12 ley line, 13 fel, 14 swamp and 18 azerite (`liquid_<name>.gdshader`, each a port of the matching `liquid*Mat.slang`). ## WMO liquids (MLIQ)

WMO group liquids were parsed but never drawn. They now follow WebWowViewerCpp (`game_engine_core::wmo_liquid`, `godot/rust/src/terrain/wmo_liquid.rs`):

- **LiquidType** (`WmoGroupObject::setLiquidType`, `wmoGroupObject.cpp:151-201`): with MOHD flag 0x4 a group liquid n below 21 is `to_wmo_liquid(n - 1)`, any other n a LiquidType ID; without it n below 20 is `to_wmo_liquid(n)`, Green Lava (15) none, the rest n + 1. `to_wmo_liquid` keeps the low two bits: WMO Water 13 (WMO Ocean 14 with MOGP 0x80000), Ocean 14, Magma 19, Slime 20. **Literal PBRWater chain behind the opt-in (2026-11-05).** The colour and mask chain now follows the Forever DXBC (`scripts/bls_dump.py 2977157`, permutation 255) line by line instead of the earlier adaptation. Registers: t0 and t1 are sampled with the first vertex texture-coordinate pair (v5.xy, v5.zw) and t2 and t3 with the second (v6.xy, v6.zw); the role order is the row's first four surface elements (high, mid, low, rim — the depth and river elements belong to the depth variant this permutation never reads). Coverage is `2*(t0*t1) + t2.wxyz`: its x component saturated is the alpha coverage and the luminance of yzw is the foam amount, `mask = coverage * dot(stack.yzw, (0.3, 0.59, 0.11))`. The water colour is `t3.rgb * tint * t3.a`, where the row colours are the client-prepared RGB triples (colour 0 already inverted into batch word 0, so no channel swap), and the tint blends colour 1 with the LightData river close colour by the row's first float, which the decompilation identifies as a blend amount against an unpublished per-channel target table (ADAPTED for that table only). The light factor multiplies the colour and every term that builds it saturates, so the factor is an attenuation in 0..1 (see the earlier Zephras note); retail also writes an alpha-to-coverage mask and discards below 0.501961, which the forward renderer replaces with the mask as a blend weight (stated deviation).

The remaining gap is hue: the fallback reads bluer, the PBR chain slightly green. All six masks measure 0.91-0.96 rgb with 0.18-0.46 alpha, so the coverage signal is in alpha and the colour comes from the tint, which is why the tint choice dominates the hue. The default stays the LiquidType5 fallback pending an eye check of the pair; `pbr_water_pixels` proves the coverage, tint and layer-UV transforms all reach the pixels.

## Why the literal chain still does not read as water — 2026-11-05

The eye test on the literal port rejected it: uniform khaki caustics, no shore band, no depth gradient toward the deep middle, while the fallback reads as water. The DXBC explains all three, and the second one was a real omission in the port.

**The depth stage was missing.** Permutation 255 does read scene depth. Line 414 samples `t10` (a `texture2d`) at the screen position, line 415 subtracts the fragment's own view depth (`v3.z`), line 416 saturates the gap times a material constant `cb0[23].x`, lines 417-419 take `log`/`mul cb0[23].y`/`exp` (so the gap is raised to a power), and line 420-421 scale it by `cb0[5].x` and a per-vertex attribute `v2.x`. The result multiplies the colour at line 459 and the alpha at line 450. That is the shore gradient: it is a scene-depth gap, not the `LiquidTypeXTexture` `_depth` art. `t16` (a `texture3d`) is a second fade volume read at lines 691 and 822. The port had bound the row's `_depth` art instead and invented a foam cubic, so it could not produce a shore band at all.

**The body colour is not in the shader.** `t3.rgb * tint * t3.a`, with the six authored masks measuring 0.91-0.96 rgb and 0.18-0.46 alpha, is a tint scaled by coverage. The row's own colours for the Zephras pairs (LiquidType 1251 lake, 1279 river, both `MaterialID` 130) decode as `Color_0` = (0.702, 0.859, 0.800) pale mint, `Color_1` = (0.984, 0.875, 0.463) cream, `Color_2` = (0.765, 0.596, 0.298) tan; none is a water blue. The LightData pair the fallback uses has **black rgb and zero alpha** for this view, which is why an opacity probe of the port measured 0 over the water: `mix(scene, lit, 0)` shows the refracted scene, and the surface reads as the bright seabed plus tan foam, exactly the khaki complaint. `Float_2 = 7.85` (the depth-mix coefficient) saturates immediately, `Float_10` = 0.003/0.01 (specular weight), `Float_11` = 0.34/0.10 (a shore width in yards, so the authored band is one or two pixels wide at this pose). The provenance of the water blue is therefore entirely in the unpublished CPU-side tint target table, which is now the outstanding question to the decompilation session together with the permutation-selection bits and the river/ocean colour selection.

**A second port of the depth stage was tried and withdrawn.** Adding the `t10` gap to the port (with the row's `Float_11` as the width and the exponent linear) and weighting the specular by `Float_10` removed the clipping (6.36% to 0.37%) and produced a shore term, but the coast region still measured 94/131/149 against the fallback's 39/53/61, because the body colour is black with zero alpha and the result is the scene plus foam. With the colour source blocked on the unpublished table, the change was reverted and the branch keeps the reviewed literal port; the default stays the fallback.

They are published as their own fragment (`model-asset-index-forever-terrain-textures-20261105.json`, 182 receipts, 18.0 MiB) — but the render did not change, because the terrain texture path does not use model receipts: `terrain/textures.rs` resolves each BLP through `CascListfileResolver::ensure_cached_checked` into `data/textures/<fdid>.blp`, and those files were already present. So the receipts close a coverage gap, not the black terrain.

Tree silhouettes were not a texture gap either: of the 461 M2s placed on map 2959, every TXID texture they reference already had a receipt (four M2 files were missing, not their textures).

## Why five Forever maps never stream a tile — 2026-11-05

`preview_world_map` derives the streaming tile from `bevy_to_tile_coords(position.x, position.z)`, i.e. `row = 32 + z / 533.33` and `col = 32 - x / 533.33`, so the point must carry the WoW world coordinate in Godot's z. Even with poses derived that way from each WDT's `MAIN`-active tiles, and with the root and `_tex0` ADT files present, maps 2807, 3021, 2853, 2998 and 2832 report `world_attached: false` with `parsed_tiles: []` and `failures: []` — the preview never queues a tile, so nothing is attempted and no error is raised. Maps 2991, 2959 and 2980 stream normally with the same arithmetic, which rules out the pose maths, the tile data and the receipts; the difference is in those maps' streaming state and is not yet located.

## The two "identical" Forever frames, and the black pond — 2026-11-05

The 2959 and 2980 captures are not crossed files. Each run reports its own map directory and pose, and each loads its own WDT and root ADTs (2959 tile `(33,32)` is FDID 6983090, 2980's is 7095904; the bytes differ). What makes the frames look alike is that the two maps are variants of one zone: 2980's 16 `MAIN`-active tiles are a subset of 2959's coordinates, so the same hillside is on both. The earlier 2959 frame was a city because that pose was tile `(31,31)`; the later one stood on tile `(33,32)`, the tile with the most doodads. The city view is `map-2959-city-fallback.jpg`, and it contains no near-black region at all (0.0% of pixels below 12/255).

The black pond belongs to the hillside pose. Two candidate causes remain open, and the evidence narrows it to the liquid path rather than the terrain: map 2959 logs two `Material130` fallback selections (LiquidType 12xx) and 73 `MH2O ... liquid layer (2, 42): Forever LiquidObject 42 has no DB2 row` drops, while 2980 logs 42 of those drops and no fallback selection. Object 42 is the ocean object that intentionally has no DB2 row, and the terrain path drops those layers instead of mapping them. The fallback water colour then comes from LightData, where 3,752 of the 5,607 Forever rows carry zero Ocean/River colour, which draws a black surface. Forcing the PBR path (row colours) changed only 0.13% of the city frame's pixels, so at that pose neither path is visibly wrong — the pond's own MH2O layer and LiquidData row still need to be dumped to close this.

## MH2O `LiquidObject` is a hybrid, and we were dropping every Forever sea — 2026-11-05

The decompilation (build 70707) settles the field: below 42 the value **is** the vertex format and is used literally with no lookup; exactly 42 is a **sentinel** meaning "no overridden vertex format", never looked up; above 42 it is a real key whose miss yields an all-zero flow record. For the sentinel the format comes from the layer's LiquidType with one hard-coded override — LiquidType 2 (Ocean) forces format 2, ignoring LiquidMaterial 1's LVF 0 — and that format draws a flat grid at `0 - baseZ` over the full 8x8 grid, ignoring the layer rectangle. The shared type-8 retry fires only when the LiquidType row itself is missing.

Our Forever path returned an error for any object at or above 42 without a row, so every ocean layer was dropped: 73 on map 2959 and 42 on map 2980. It now applies the hybrid rule (and the Retail path uses the same code), which removes all 73 and 42 drops with the object counts unchanged (2959 4950 spawned, 2980 4159 spawned, both 0 failures). `liquid_object_is_a_hybrid_of_format_key_and_sentinel` pins the three cases; `forever_ocean_object_42_takes_the_layer_liquid_type` pins ocean 42 to LiquidType 2, format 2, zero flow.

## The capture harness was running the wrong `.so` — 2026-11-05

Every preview capture today loaded `target/release/libgame_engine_godot.so`, not the debug library the builds produce: the pinned Godot is a *release* template, so `linux.release.x86_64` in `game_engine.gdextension` wins. Proof: `grep libgame_engine_godot /proc/<pid>/maps` on a running capture shows the release path. That is why a rebuilt debug library appeared to have no effect, and why `terrain_state()` looked unchanged after adding keys to it.

The release path cannot be rebuilt in this tree: `cargo build --release` fails with four `no method named census_clear/census_prepare/census_start` errors in `spell_effects::SpellEffects`, so the release artefact was from 11:48 and predated every change made today. Copying the debug library over the release path is the local verification workaround used for the object-42 capture; the real fix is to make those `census_*` methods available in release or to stop shipping a release-only template.

## LightData's water colours are dead data — 2026-11-05

The client never reads `OceanCloseColor`, `OceanFarColor`, `RiverCloseColor` or `RiverFarColor`: there is no reader, no zero test, no default and no inheritance, and all 5,607 rows carry alpha 0 because the columns are `0x00RRGGBB`. Our fallback water still sources its close/far pair from those columns, so a zone on an all-zero row (410 of 837 light-parameter profiles) draws black water. Our alpha is not the trap — it comes from LightParams, not from the colour word — which is why the surface reads black rather than invisible. The colours are being removed from the water path once the material-1 colour source is known; the PBR path already uses the LiquidType row's own triple.

## Release-library A/B, and where the silent non-attachment actually lives — 2026-11-05

Master fixed the release build (`spell_effects::census_*` is no longer debug-only), so the object-42 change is now measured on real release artefacts. Building `db1116922` and the rebased fix separately gives two libraries that differ only by that change: the pre-fix library still contains the `Forever LiquidObject` error string, the post-fix one does not. Chatting the pose `(-800, 100, 266.7)` through both, with `/proc/<pid>/maps` confirming `target/release/libgame_engine_godot.so` each time: map 2959 drops 73 ocean layers before and 0 after, map 2980 42 before and 0 after, with object counts unchanged (4950 and 4159 spawned, 0 failures, 6 and 5 tiles). The debug library copied over the release path has been removed; the release artefact now matches the branch.

The silent non-attachment is located but not fixed. `StreamedTerrain::queue_tile` returns `Ok(())` without a word when the requested tile is not in `wdt.tiles.active`, and the two sides use opposite tuple orders: the streaming side passes `(row, col)` from `bevy_to_tile_coords`, while `WdtTiles::active` is filled as `(index % 64, index / 64)` — `(col, row)`. Maps whose active block is symmetric about the diagonal (2959, 2980, 2991) match by accident; 3021, 2807, 2853, 2998 and 2832 never do, and the preview reports `world_attached` with no tiles, no failures and no error. Adding the transposed containment test did **not** change the behaviour on those five maps, so the transposition is not the whole story and that change was reverted rather than landed. The diagnostic fields (`pending_map`, `pending_tiles`, `global_wmo_present`) also identified the state: the map request completes, the WDT reports no global WMO, and no tile is ever queued — so the remaining question is which coordinate convention reaches `queue_tile` for those maps.

## Legacy water colour is an environment record, not LightData — 2026-11-05

Material-1 water reads two colour records from the per-view environment object — `{ 0xAARRGGBB colour, an int, three floats }`, one of two offset pairs chosen by a runtime predicate (ocean vs river, INFERRED), converted by bytes 2,1,0,3 over 255 with alpha included and staged into five constant slots. LightData's four water colours are not read by the client at all, and our pipeline's alpha comes from LightParams rather than the `0x00RRGGBB` word, which is why a zero row renders as a black surface instead of as nothing. Who fills those environment records per view is still unknown, so the fallback keeps its current behaviour and the gap is recorded rather than papered over with an invented colour.
